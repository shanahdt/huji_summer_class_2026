Three questions for the next eight days#

What kind of knowledge does corpus analysis produce?

Every method encodes assumptions about what music is,
what matters about it, and whose music counts as data.

Pause here. Ask: what do you think counts as data in musicology? This is the question we’ll return to every single day.

How can we ask questions that are musicologically meaningful?

The distance between frequency and meaning is often a large one.

Just because something appears a lot doesn’t mean it matters. Scale degree 5 is the most common in almost everything — so what?

Ask questions that are specifically of interest to you.

Fluency in Python is not the goal. A good research question is.

This is the most important slide. The tools are in service of curiosity. Tell them: by Day 5 you should have a research question you care about.

Primary corpora#

  1. Beregovski Klezmer Corpus — Malin & Shanahan (2025)

  2. Essen Folksong Collection — Schaffrath & Huron (1995)

  3. Meertens Tune Collection — Van Kranenburg et al. (2014)

  4. Bach Chorales — encoded by David Huron

  5. McGill Billboard Corpus — Burgoyne (2012)

Bring your own corpus — we can work with it.

We’ll use Beregovski most heavily — 245 klezmer tunes in kern format. The others come in later sessions. Mention the klezmer connection to Israel.
